# CODEX PROMPT: Cellarium Anatomical Reconstruction, Genetic Tissue Development, and Natural Growth

**Repository:** `markchpaman10/cellarium`  
**Branch:** `desktop-laboratory-dev`

## OBJECTIVE

Rebuild Cellarium's organism generator around genetically controlled anatomical components, skeletal structures, layered tissues, and natural biological growth.

The previous generator produced nearly identical dog-shaped organisms with segmented oval bodies, thin limbs, duplicated heads, and simplified anatomy.

Some detailed skeletal, muscular, fat, nervous, and organ coverage from the earlier biological system also appears to have regressed.

We need to correct the underlying biological system, not simply improve the artwork.

**The intended anatomical construction process is:**

DNA → Body regions → Skeletal framework → Organs and nerves → Muscles → Fat → Skin → Mature organism

These systems must develop together over time, respecting biological dependencies.

**CRITICAL REQUIREMENTS:**

1. Restore the detailed anatomy and tissue coverage from the previous system.
2. Define reusable anatomical components rather than complete animal templates.
3. Allow DNA to determine component dimensions, proportions, quantities, and tissue properties.
4. Derive physical statistics from actual developed anatomy.
5. Generate a continuous external body surface from the underlying tissues.
6. Make embryonic and juvenile growth gradual, organic, and visually continuous.
7. Preserve the existing laboratory, genome, physiology, habitat, and persistence systems.

This is still experimental development. Prioritize functional biological simulation over cosmetic polish.

## 2. CREATE REUSABLE ANATOMICAL COMPONENTS

We need predefined anatomical building blocks, NOT predefined animal species.

Each component must have genetically adjustable dimensions, proportions, connections, and development rules.

### Torso

Support:

- Spine or alternative central structural support
- Vertebrae or connected structural segments
- Rib cage or equivalent protective structures
- Chest cavity
- Abdominal cavity
- Shoulder attachment points
- Hip and pelvic attachment points
- Variable length, width, thickness, curvature, and orientation

The torso must not automatically become a horizontal chain of overlapping circles.

DNA determines its structure and proportions.

### Neck

Support:

- Connected skeletal or flexible segments
- Variable segment count
- Variable length
- Variable thickness
- Curvature
- Joint flexibility
- Muscle coverage

Necks may be short, long, absent, or repeated where the body plan supports them.

### Head and skull

Generate actual skull structures with:

- Cranium
- Upper jaw
- Lower jaw
- Jaw hinge
- Snout or muzzle
- Eye sockets
- Mouth opening
- Tooth attachment points
- Muscle attachment points

Skull genetics must determine visible head and mouth shape.

For example:

- An elongated skull produces an elongated snout.
- A short, broad skull produces a broader head.
- Jaw proportions affect mouth opening and potential bite force.

Multiple heads must have their own skeletal and nervous connections.

Do not duplicate decorative head graphics.

### Arms

Define:

- Upper arm
- Elbow
- Forearm
- Wrist
- Hand

### Legs

Define:

- Upper leg
- Knee
- Lower leg
- Ankle
- Foot

Each segment must support genetically controlled:

- Length
- Width
- Thickness
- Bone density
- Curvature
- Orientation
- Joint movement limits
- Muscle attachment locations

The underlying appendage system should be reusable.

Arms and legs are functional specializations of articulated appendages, not completely unrelated systems.

### Hands, feet, fingers, and toes

Support:

- Palm or foot base
- Variable digit count
- Individual digit bones
- Variable digit segment count
- Digit joints
- Claws or nails
- Genetically controlled terminal structures

Claws must develop from appropriate terminal anatomy.

Their length, curvature, strength, and effectiveness depend on supporting bones and claw-development genetics.

Do not draw random spikes and label them claws.

### Tails and other appendages

Support:

- Segmented tails
- Flexible extensions
- Fins
- Wings
- Antennae
- Horns
- Other anatomically supported structures

Each must connect to valid body regions and develop appropriate supporting tissues.

## 4. GENERATE THE SKELETON AS THE STRUCTURAL FOUNDATION

The skeleton must establish the organism's physical framework.

Each bone or equivalent supporting structure requires:

- Unique anatomical ID
- Parent and child connections
- Length
- Width
- Density
- Mass
- Orientation
- Joint attachment points
- Muscle attachment points
- Structural integrity
- Growth state

Joints must define:

- Connected structures
- Movement range
- Orientation
- Stability
- Supporting tissues

Bones must not float independently.

The skeletal system determines basic body shape, proportions, and movement possibilities.

**Do not generate a finished silhouette and place decorative bones inside it.**

## 5. DEVELOP ORGANS INSIDE THE BODY

Use the existing organ and physiology systems.

Place organs inside suitable anatomical regions according to:

- Available body volume
- Skeletal cavities
- Genetic instructions
- Functional requirements
- Other organ locations
- Supporting connective tissues

Support respiratory, digestive, circulatory, reproductive, and nervous structures where appropriate.

## 6. RESTORE AND DEVELOP INTERCONNECTED NERVES

The nervous system must follow the actual anatomy.

Nerves should branch between:

- Central nervous structures
- Sensory organs
- Muscles
- Limbs
- Digits
- Internal organs

Nerve density, branching, and development are genetically controlled.

Nerves must intertwine through appropriate tissues rather than appear as unrelated decorative lines.

Motor control and sensory performance should depend on the actual nervous-system connections.

---

## 7. GROW MUSCLES AROUND THE SKELETON

Muscles must be actual anatomical tissue structures.

Each muscle requires:

- Attachment points
- Volume
- Density
- Mass
- Orientation
- Functional role
- Growth state

Genetics controls muscle density, maximum growth potential, and regional distribution.

Muscle development must follow the skeletal framework.

Examples:

- Thick leg bones with substantial muscle development produce visibly powerful legs.
- Thin skeletal segments with limited muscle development produce slender limbs.
- Strong jaw muscles visibly affect the head and jaw region.

Muscles must connect to structures they can physically move.

Muscle mass contributes to total body mass and physical capabilities.

---

## 8. DEVELOP FAT ACCORDING TO GENETICS AND PHYSIOLOGY

Fat develops around and between appropriate tissues.

Genetics determines:

- Regional distribution
- Storage capacity
- Preferred thickness
- Storage efficiency

Actual fat reserves depend on nutrition, age, metabolism, and energy balance.

Fat must visibly affect the organism's external shape.

An organism with substantial fat reserves should look different from an otherwise identical lean organism.

Distinguish tissue density, tissue volume, and tissue mass.

---

## 9. GENERATE SKIN OVER THE DEVELOPED ANATOMY

Skin must follow the outer envelope of the actual tissues.

Genetics controls:

- Thickness
- Toughness
- Elasticity
- Protective adaptations
- Surface characteristics

Support scales, armor, hardened skin, and other existing protective tissues.

The final external silhouette must be derived from skeletal geometry, muscle volume, fat distribution, and skin thickness.

**Stop drawing organisms as overlapping circles and ovals.**

Adjacent anatomical structures must blend into a continuous body surface while preserving recognizable joints, necks, shoulders, hips, and other features.

### Endurance

- Organ capacity

- Circulation

## 11. IMPLEMENT BIOLOGICAL TRADEOFFS

Examples:

- Dense bones improve structural strength but increase body mass.
- Larger muscles produce more force but consume more energy.
- Thick armor improves protection but may reduce mobility.
- Longer limbs change stride length and mechanical leverage.
- Larger heads require greater neck support.
- Additional limbs require more muscles, nerves, and metabolic resources.
- Greater fat reserves provide stored energy but increase weight.

Use actual anatomical and physiological calculations wherever practical.

## 12. NATURAL AND CONTINUOUS GROWTH

**THIS IS A MAJOR REQUIREMENT.**

Currently, anatomical development sometimes appears to happen in sudden jumps.

A limb may abruptly appear, a body region may instantly change size, or a fully developed structure may replace an earlier shape.

This must be corrected.

We need organisms to visibly grow through continuous biological development.

### 12.1 Persistent developing structures

Every anatomical structure must maintain a persistent identity throughout development.

Do not repeatedly destroy and recreate the entire anatomy at different growth stages.

Each structure should track:

- Developmental activation
- Current size
- Target size
- Growth progress
- Tissue maturity
- Functional maturity
- Genetic growth potential
- Nutritional and environmental constraints

When a new structure develops, it should begin as a small anatomical bud or precursor.

It should not suddenly appear at full size.

### 12.2 Gradual limb development

For example, a developing leg should:

1. Begin as a small limb bud.
2. Gradually extend away from the torso.
3. Establish its supporting skeletal structures.
4. Elongate its upper and lower segments.
5. Develop a visible joint.
6. Form a foot bud.
7. Gradually develop individual toes.
8. Accumulate muscle tissue.
9. Develop nerves, connective tissues, and circulation.
10. Accumulate fat and external covering.
11. Continue growing toward mature proportions.

These processes may overlap.

Do not treat this list as a requirement to finish one tissue completely before beginning another.

The goal is biologically plausible, continuous development.

### 12.3 Gradual head and torso development

The torso must expand and differentiate continuously.

The head should develop from an identifiable region rather than suddenly appearing as a completed skull.

Jaw structures should extend and differentiate gradually.

Neck segments should elongate progressively.

Additional heads or appendages must emerge from developmental regions and grow over time.

### 12.4 Continuous skeletal growth

Bone lengths, widths, and density should change incrementally.

Joints and attached tissues must update with the growing skeleton.

Growth must preserve anatomical connectivity.

A growing limb must not detach from its torso or leave muscles behind.

### 12.5 Continuous tissue growth

Muscle, fat, skin, nerves, and organs must develop progressively.

Their volumes and shapes should change as the organism grows.

For example:

- Muscle mass increases gradually around developing bones.
- Fat accumulates over time according to energy availability.
- Skin expands with the underlying tissues.
- Nerves extend into growing anatomical regions.
- Organs enlarge and mature as body demands increase.

### 12.6 Separate biological growth from rendering

The simulation must store actual anatomical growth states.

The renderer should interpolate smoothly between valid simulation states.

Do not implement fake growth by simply scaling a finished organism.

Do not use visual interpolation to conceal incorrect simulation state transitions.

Both the biological simulation and its visual representation must support continuous development.

### 12.7 Developmental timing

Genetics must influence:

- Growth rate
- Relative timing of anatomical structures
- Maturation speed
- Maximum tissue development
- Juvenile-to-adult proportions

Nutrition, temperature, oxygen, and other relevant environmental conditions may affect growth.

Growth should use simulation time rather than frame count.

The same organism must develop consistently regardless of rendering frame rate.

### 12.8 Growth animation

The Anatomy Generator playback must show smooth growth at normal and accelerated speeds.

Provide:

- Play
- Pause
- Step
- Restart
- Playback speed controls
- Development timeline

At normal speed, anatomical changes should appear gradual.

At accelerated speed, the organism should grow faster without skipping essential developmental states.

Stepping through development should reveal actual intermediate anatomy.

### 12.9 Growth continuity tests

Add automated checks to detect:

- Sudden unexplained changes in structure dimensions
- Instant appearance of fully mature appendages
- Loss of anatomical connections during growth
- Disconnected muscles or nerves
- Abrupt changes in tissue mass
- Skin envelopes failing to follow growing anatomy
- Growth differences caused by rendering frame rate

Some biological events, such as tissue differentiation or activation of a developmental program, may be discrete. Their resulting physical growth must still be represented appropriately.

**Acceptance requirement: Watching an organism develop should look like watching something grow, not watching a collection of body parts pop into existence.**
